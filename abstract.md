# Abstract (Version A)

This study investigates changes in individual autonomy and family dependence among Indian youth across multiple generations. Existing literature on individualism and collectivism in India has largely relied on qualitative interpretations of cultural and social change. This study adopts a quantitative approach by constructing measurable indicators of autonomy, mobility, family dependence, and household structure.

Using survey data collected across different age cohorts, the study examines variations in educational decision-making, employment mobility, residential independence, family obligations, and preferences regarding family structure. Statistical analyses are used to compare cohorts, regions, and socioeconomic groups in order to identify long-term trends associated with economic liberalization, urbanization, and changing labor markets.

The study aims to determine whether measurable shifts toward greater personal autonomy have occurred in post-liberalization India and to evaluate the extent to which traditional family-centered institutions continue to influence individual life choices. The findings contribute to the growing literature on social transformation, demographic change, and intergenerational value shifts in contemporary India.

**Can Indian Youth Afford Individual Autonomy? A Quantitative Study of Economic Independence Across Generations**

---

# Abstract (Version B)

This study examines the economic conditions that enable or constrain independent living among Indian workers across different generations. While discussions of individualism and changing family structures are often framed in cultural or ideological terms, this study approaches the issue through measurable economic indicators.

Using cohort-based survey data, the research analyzes trends in wages, household expenditure, housing affordability, dependency burdens, family transfers, and intergenerational support. Particular attention is given to the relationship between real income growth and the rising costs of housing and household formation. The study evaluates whether economic independence has become more or less attainable for successive generations despite broader economic development.

By comparing workers who entered the labor force during different periods of India's post-liberalization era, the study seeks to identify the structural factors that influence independent living, family dependence, and long-term economic mobility. The findings contribute to debates on housing affordability, labor market outcomes, demographic change, and the future of family-based social support systems in India.
